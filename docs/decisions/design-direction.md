# Design direction: where sources disagree and what we chose

Sources: Anthropic `frontend-design`, Taste Skill (`design-taste-frontend` v2, incl. `app-screens.md`, `tells.md`), Interface Design. Commits pinned in NOTICE.md.

| # | Topic | What sources say | Our rule | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "AI look" patterns | Taste Skill: hard bans enforced by a browser inspector. Frontend Design: they are defaults, not choices; the brief's own words win. Interface Design: "avoid" list | **Defaults that need a reason, not bans.** The brief always wins. `ui-review` flags them when no reason is given. | Hard bans age badly (Taste Skill's own registry shows each "fix" becoming the next tell) and break legitimate briefs. |
| 2 | Shadows, borders, rules | Taste Skill: no shadows at rest, no hairlines. Interface Design: quiet rgba borders, layered shadows for lift, pick one depth strategy | **Pick one depth strategy per product** (borders, subtle shadows, or tone steps). Shadows always fine on things that float (menus, dialogs). Quiet dividers are normal in tables, forms and dense tools. | Interface Design's "commit to one" is the useful rule; Taste Skill's own exemptions (tables, forms, real UI) point the same way. |
| 3 | Motion on pages | Taste Skill: required motion layer, entrance on every section, ≥ 8 moving elements. Frontend Design: one orchestrated moment; fade-up on every section reads as generated | **One signature moment; motion that answers the user's actions. Per-section entrances optional and subtle.** Values come from the `motion` skill. | Matches Frontend Design and our motion skill's restraint; a minimum count of moving elements optimises for a juror, not a user. |
| 4 | Choosing typefaces | Frontend Design and Interface Design: avoid reaching for the usual default. Our `typography` skill: never propose a new face unless the task is about type | **In new work or an explicit redesign, choosing faces is in scope: pick deliberately, say why. For an existing product, keep the brand's faces.** | The two skills apply at different moments. |
| 5 | Asking vs deciding | Taste Skill: decide and state the reading. Interface Design: ask one question if vague. Frontend Design: propose subject/audience/job and confirm | **State assumptions and proceed. Ask one question only when the answer would change the direction substantially or is costly to undo.** | Keeps momentum without guessing on high-stakes choices. |
| 6 | Imagery | Taste Skill: at least four real pictures per page, sourced from open collections when the client has none | **Show the real product or real subject when it exists. Never fake proof** (customers, quotes, logos, statistics, product shots). Found imagery is credited and never presented as the client's own. No minimum count. | Honesty rule kept; the count is dropped. |
| 7 | Specific colours called out (cream + terracotta, violet/indigo, black + neon) | Taste Skill bans; Frontend Design lists as common generated looks | **Listed in `defaults.md` as common generated looks to avoid unless the brief or brand calls for them.** | Same as rule 1. |
