# Pages

Things people read and scroll: landing pages, marketing and product sites, portfolios, events.

## Write the argument first

Before design, list the page as **claims with their proof**, one line per section. The claims decide which sections exist and in what order. A section with no claim is deleted. This avoids the stock sequence (hero, logos, three features, testimonial, pricing, CTA).

## The idea

Fill in this sentence from the subject's inventory, not the category:

> On this page, **[something only this subject has]** becomes **[the device that carries the page]**.

Places to look: what the product produces, its physical shape, its name, its place, its ritual or sequence, the printed artefacts of its world, the feeling of a different category it could borrow. Run the competitor test: if the sentence still works with a competitor's name in it, keep looking.

Optionally give the page **one thing the visitor can do** that comes from the idea: set the amount and see the real monthly figure, choose a size and see it in a room. It must use real content, work on a phone, never block reading, and have a plain fallback.

## The first screen

- Answers the visitor's first question in large type: **what this is**, and what it costs, does or when and where it happens.
- **A clear title** (the page's one `h1`), one supporting sentence directly beneath it, and one primary action named for what it does.
- **Headlines fit in two lines** at every width. Cut words first, widen second, shrink last.
- Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world. "Big number, small label, gradient accent" is a default; use it only when it's truly the best option.

## Sections

- **Full content.** Every section the visitor came for delivers its information completely (the whole menu, every plan's inclusions, a real answer per question). Thin sections lose to busier, honest ones.
- **Neighbouring sections don't share a layout.** Carry the idea into every section, including pricing, FAQ and footer.
- **Every section has a heading** that names it.
- **One grid, equal outer margins.** Asymmetry lives inside the grid; an empty half of the screen is closed by something on the far side.
- **Gaps between sections clearly larger than gaps inside them.**
- **At most one deliberate empty pause** per page.
- **Statements set large** stay short (about 25 words). Larger than that, they're headlines and must fit in two lines.

## Imagery

- **Prefer, in order:** the product's own pictures or real interface; generated imagery when a tool exists; real, credited pictures of the subject's world; drawings made for this subject; type and colour alone.
- One dominant image beats several small ones. Keep crops and treatments consistent.
- **Never fake proof:** no invented customers, quotes, reviews, logos, press or statistics. Found imagery is credited and never presented as the client's own product, team or place.
- Nothing sits on top of a headline.

## Copy

- Name things the way users understand them, not how the system is built.
- Buttons say exactly what happens ("Book a table", not "Submit"), and the same action keeps the same name through the flow.
- Errors explain what happened and how to fix it; empty states invite the next action.
- Placeholder content is fine where the brief leaves gaps; list it for the owner to replace.

## Motion

One signature moment, plus feedback on the visitor's own actions. Every section fading up on scroll is a default, not a choice. Use the `motion` skill for values.
